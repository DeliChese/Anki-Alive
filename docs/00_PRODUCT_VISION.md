# Anki Alive — Product Vision

## 1. Vision

Anki Alive transforms Anki from a queue of due cards into a living memory system that users feel compelled to return to, explore, and complete.

The product should make studying feel less like clearing administrative debt and more like interacting with an evolving record of one's own memory.

The central idea is simple:

> **The learner's memory is the game world.**

The add-on should not fabricate progress where no learning has occurred. It should surface, dramatize, and visualize real changes in memory stability, difficulty, survival, fragility, recovery, and long-term retention.

---

## 2. The Problem

Anki is extremely effective, but the experience can feel emotionally flat.

Typical study sessions are dominated by:

- due counts,
- repetitive card presentation,
- abstract scheduling,
- little sense of narrative,
- weak session closure,
- minimal anticipation before opening the app,
- little emotional distinction between an ordinary card and a personally meaningful one.

This creates a motivation gap.

A learner may know that Anki is useful while still feeling resistance to opening it.

A learner may open Anki but stop after only a few cards.

A learner may avoid especially difficult or dry material even when that material is precisely what needs attention.

The product opportunity is not to replace spaced repetition.

The opportunity is to make real spaced-repetition progress feel vivid enough that the learner wants to continue.

---

## 3. Desired User Experience

Anki Alive should create three simultaneous motivational layers.

### 3.1 Immediate Curiosity

When the learner opens Anki, there should be unresolved questions.

Examples:

- Which memories are fragile today?
- Which card has become a Nemesis?
- Did the Oracle correctly predict a failure?
- What is hidden later in the Expedition?
- Is a Relic about to form?
- What changed since yesterday?

The learner should feel:

> "There is something here I want to discover."

### 3.2 Session Completion Tension

Once a study session starts, the learner should feel meaningful progress toward closure.

The session should not feel like an endless queue.

Progress should be divided into psychologically manageable segments:

- short checkpoints,
- unresolved events,
- nearby reveals,
- visible session goals,
- meaningful completion states.

The learner should frequently feel:

> "I am close to finishing this part."

This is not intended to create an infinite engagement loop.

The product should lead toward closure, not continually move the finish line.

### 3.3 Long-Term Ownership

Over weeks, months, and years, the learner should build a persistent history of memory.

Examples include:

- Relics,
- old memories,
- defeated Nemeses,
- rescued fragile memories,
- long-lived knowledge,
- meaningful milestones,
- an evolving Memory World.

The learner should feel:

> "This collection represents what I have genuinely learned."

---

## 4. Core Experience Loop

The core product loop is:

**Open → Expedition → Recall → Event → Closure → Long-term memory history**

### Open

The learner sees a concise overview of the current memory state and today's unresolved signals.

### Expedition

The study session becomes a bounded journey with meaningful progress and checkpoints.

### Recall

The learner performs the real cognitive work.

This remains the center of the product.

### Event

The system reveals meaningful events derived from memory data.

Examples:

- Oracle result,
- Rescue opportunity,
- Nemesis encounter,
- Fragment recovery,
- Relic formation.

### Closure

The learner reaches a real completion point.

The product should celebrate completion without immediately manufacturing a new obligation.

### Long-Term Memory History

The outcome becomes part of a persistent personal memory narrative.

---

## 5. Product Promise

Anki Alive promises to make real learning feel more alive without corrupting the learning process.

That means:

- no fake progress,
- no punishment for honest forgetting,
- no need to lie to the scheduler,
- no arbitrary currency economy,
- no requirement to maintain a streak,
- no forced engagement after a session is complete.

The product earns motivation from the learner's own memory data.

---

## 6. What Makes Anki Alive Different

Many productivity and learning products add motivation using external rewards.

Examples include:

- XP,
- coins,
- streaks,
- badges,
- levels,
- leaderboards,
- daily quests.

Anki Alive takes a different approach.

The product treats existing learning data as meaningful material.

For example:

- a difficult card becomes a Nemesis,
- a declining memory becomes a Rescue opportunity,
- a long-lived memory becomes a Relic,
- a prediction becomes an Oracle challenge,
- a session becomes an Expedition,
- old review history becomes a personal archive.

The system should feel personalized because it is built from the learner's actual history.

---

## 7. The Role of Dopamine and Behavioral Design

Anki Alive may use behavioral design techniques, but they must serve learning.

Useful mechanisms include:

- curiosity,
- anticipation,
- visible progress,
- near-completion tension,
- variable discovery,
- ownership,
- mastery,
- recovery,
- nostalgia,
- challenge,
- meaningful loss and restoration.

These mechanisms must never be used to manipulate the learner into endless interaction.

The intended loop is:

> **Anticipation → Effort → Recall → Meaningful feedback → Closure**

Not:

> **Anticipation → Click → Reward → More clicking**

---

## 8. Relationship With Difficult Material

Anki Alive should become more interesting when the material is difficult.

Dry or difficult cards should not be visually ignored.

They should have a chance to become:

- Rescue targets,
- Nemesis cards,
- high-value recovery moments,
- future Relics,
- memorable turning points in the learner's history.

The system should communicate:

> Difficulty is not an interruption to the experience. Difficulty is part of the experience.

---

## 9. Relationship With Failure

Forgetting is expected in spaced repetition.

Therefore:

- `Again` is information,
- a lapse is useful evidence,
- a fragile memory is a learning opportunity,
- a failed recall may create a future recovery story.

Failure must not create shame.

It may create tension, consequence, or a new objective, but it should always point toward learning.

---

## 10. Visual Vision

The working visual direction is:

> **Dark Arcane + Modern Minimal**

The interface should feel:

- atmospheric,
- precise,
- intelligent,
- calm,
- slightly mysterious,
- premium,
- contemporary.

It should avoid feeling:

- childish,
- noisy,
- casino-like,
- fantasy-heavy,
- cluttered,
- excessively animated,
- visually disconnected from Anki.

The product may use mystery and symbolism, but the underlying information must remain clear.

---

## 11. Emotional Tone

Anki Alive should create a mixture of:

- curiosity,
- calm challenge,
- progress,
- ownership,
- relief,
- satisfaction,
- occasional surprise,
- long-term pride.

It should avoid relying on:

- guilt,
- fear,
- shame,
- urgency without purpose,
- streak anxiety,
- artificial scarcity,
- social comparison.

---

## 12. Focus Mode

Motivation layers must remain optional.

A learner may sometimes want:

- exam-crunch mode,
- distraction-free review,
- reduced animation,
- minimal event interruption,
- plain progress.

Focus Mode must preserve the learning workflow without making the user feel that they are using an inferior version of the product.

---

## 13. Success Criteria

Anki Alive is successful if it improves behavior without degrading learning integrity.

Important qualitative outcomes include:

- users are more willing to open Anki,
- users complete more meaningful review sessions,
- users are less likely to stop after a few cards,
- difficult cards feel worth engaging with,
- users care about long-term memory history,
- users do not feel pressured to lie with `Good` or `Easy`,
- users can explain why a reward occurred,
- users can complete a session and stop without feeling manipulated.

Potential quantitative metrics may later include:

- expedition completion rate,
- session continuation after first checkpoint,
- review honesty indicators,
- focus mode usage,
- rescue completion,
- return rate,
- long-term retention proxies,
- voluntary session completion.

Metrics must never become the product's master.

---

## 14. Non-Goals

Anki Alive is not intended to:

- replace Anki's scheduler,
- replace FSRS,
- become a general-purpose game platform,
- become a social leaderboard product,
- create a virtual economy,
- pressure users into daily streaks,
- reward time spent for its own sake,
- maximize session length,
- maximize notification clicks,
- make the user dependent on visual effects to study.

---

## 15. North Star

When product decisions become difficult, ask:

> **Does this make genuine remembering more compelling?**

If the answer is yes, the idea may belong.

If the idea mainly makes clicking, collecting, or staying longer more compelling, it probably does not.
